# Story in short form

A clip is not "the loud part". It is a small story: someone wants something, something gets
in the way, something happens.

## The shape of a 30-60 s clip

1. **Hook (0-2 s).** The most intriguing line or image, out of order if needed. A question
   the viewer wants answered: "he bet $100 he could..." Not a greeting, not the set-up.
2. **Set-up (the question, the stakes).** What is at stake and for whom. Pull it from wherever
   it was said, even minutes earlier.
3. **Build.** Escalation. Cut the dead time between attempts; keep the attempts.
4. **Payoff.** The moment. Give it room: slow down the cutting just before it.
5. **Button.** A reaction, a punchline, or a hard cut to black. Leave while it is alive.

## Techniques

- **Open loop.** Show the result first (or hint at it), then go back. The viewer stays to see
  how it happened.
- **Set-up from elsewhere.** If the payoff depends on something said earlier, bring that line
  in as its own segment, or flash it as context with a label.
- **Context labels.** When the picture does not explain itself (who is winning, what the
  scoreboard means, who this person is), say it in at most four words on screen: "0:59 0-0",
  "WORLD #1", "LAST LIFE". Labels must change when the facts change (a goal is scored).
- **Title.** At most six words, says what is about to happen without spoiling it.
- **Punchline ending.** Only when the abrupt end is the joke.

## When nobody talks

Games, sports and concentrated streamers go quiet exactly when it matters. Plan from the
picture: scoreboard, clock, replays, kill feed, faces. Sound peaks (crowd, casters) tell you
when; frames tell you what. A highlight is build-up, moment and aftermath (replay or
reaction): start before the possession or the attempt began.

## Gameplay highlights

What the genre expects (Rocket League and any game with a score):

- **Outcome or nothing.** Every clip contains a payoff: a goal, a save, a demolition, an
  aerial, a near miss. Find goals by the scoreboard (`clips score`), not by guessing.
- **Never stop before the ball arrives.** Cutting away while the player drives at the ball
  is edging the viewer. If the outcome is not in the segment, the segment is not finished.
- **Play, impact, reaction.** Start at the start of the possession (kickoff, the clear, the
  pass), give the impact weight (punch-in, shake, a frame of flash, an impact sound), and
  keep the goal explosion, the celebration or the replay.
- **Transitions that move.** Zoom-throughs and whips with motion blur between plays, hard
  cuts inside a play. A dip to black between plays reads as a slideshow.
- **Drop the dead air.** Rotations, boost collecting and driving around are not story.

## Length

Aim for what the user asked for. A 60 s story needs several segments; one 60 s chunk of
stream is not a story. If there is not enough story for 60 s, make it 30 s and say so.
