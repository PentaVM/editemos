# Camera language

Words to read footage with and to ask for shots precisely.

## Shot size

- **Extreme wide / establishing.** Where we are. Small figures, big world.
- **Wide (full).** The whole body and its surroundings; action reads clearly.
- **Medium.** Waist up. Conversation, presenting.
- **Close-up.** Face. Emotion, reaction.
- **Extreme close-up / insert.** A detail: eyes, a hand, a screen, a scoreboard. Inserts carry
  context cheaply.

## Angle and height

- **Eye level.** Neutral.
- **Low angle.** Looking up at the subject: power, threat, heroism.
- **High angle.** Looking down: vulnerability, overview.
- **Dutch (tilted).** Unease. Use rarely.
- **Over the shoulder / POV.** Puts the viewer in a person's place.

## Lens

- **Wide lens (short focal length).** Exaggerated depth, distortion close up, energy.
- **Long lens (telephoto).** Compressed depth, isolated subject, observational.
- **Shallow depth of field.** One plane sharp, the rest soft: directs attention.

## Movement

- **Static.** Lets the action move.
- **Pan / tilt.** Turning on the spot: follow or reveal.
- **Dolly / truck / push-in / pull-out.** The camera travels. A slow push-in raises tension.
- **Handheld.** Urgency, presence. **Gimbal / steadicam.** Floating, smooth following.
- **Crane / drone.** Height and scale.
- **Whip pan.** Fast blur between subjects; doubles as a transition.

## Composition

- Rule of thirds as a default, centre framing for symmetry and confrontation.
- Lead room in the direction the subject looks or moves.
- Headroom small in close-ups. Vertical (9:16) framing: the subject in the upper-middle third,
  leaving the bottom for captions and platform buttons.

## Using it

When you describe a shot (for an insert, a storyboard or a generative prompt), say size,
angle, lens feel, movement and light in one line: "close-up, low angle, long lens, slow
push-in, warm rim light".
