# Colour

## Order of work

1. **Correct.** Exposure, white balance and contrast so each shot is neutral and the shots
   match each other. Match first, style second.
2. **Grade.** The look: a palette, a contrast curve, a mood.
3. **Check.** On a calibrated screen if possible; at least on a phone, where most people will
   see it.

## The look in numbers

A look can be described and measured (`reference` analyse reports these per shot and
overall):

- **Brightness / key.** Low-key (dark, mean luma under ~0.28) reads moody; high-key (over
  ~0.55) reads light and open.
- **Contrast.** The spread of brightness in the frame. Crushed blacks and bright highlights
  read punchy; a flat image reads soft, "log" or vintage.
- **Saturation.** Desaturated (under ~0.18) reads serious or documentary; saturated (over
  ~0.45) reads energetic, playful, commercial.
- **White balance / warmth.** Warm (red over blue) for comfort, nostalgia, sunset; cool for
  night, tech, tension.
- **Palette.** The few colours that dominate. A strong look usually has two: one for the
  shadows, one for the highlights (teal and orange is the over-used example).

## Rules that hold

- Skin must stay believable. Push the world around it, not the face.
- Keep the brand's colour where the brand is (graphics, titles), and do not let a grade
  contaminate it: graphics are composited after the grade.
- Shots of one scene must match before any style is added; a mismatch reads as an error.
- A LUT is a starting point. Exposure and white balance must be right before it, or it
  amplifies the error.
